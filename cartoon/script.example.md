## S1 — The click
{Mei|May} fills the kettle, flicks the switch, and walks away. A few minutes later: click. It turns itself off, right when the water boils. No screen, no chip, no app. So how does a simple kettle know when to stop?

## S2 — Follow the steam
When water reaches {100 °C|a hundred degrees Celsius} at sea level, it boils hard and makes lots of steam. A small tube carries some of that steam down towards the handle. There, it warms a thin disc made of two different metals stuck together.

## S3 — The snapping disc
The two metals grow by different amounts as they warm up. So the disc bends, until suddenly it snaps the other way. That snap pushes the switch and cuts the power. Ben calls it a tiny bodyguard in your kitchen. {Mei|May} just calls it the click.
